## Top 8 improvements, by score impact

**1. Record and link the demo video — every criterion.**
The Devpost draft still has `Video: _(YouTube link)_` as a placeholder. Most judges watch the video first; if none exists at submission, Technological Implementation, Design and Impact all get judged from text. Why: it's the single highest-leverage artifact missing, and it lets the demo show the moment-solved story the Impact criterion demands.

**2. Add AR try-on (YouCam VTO) on the tinted products — Technological Implementation, Quality of Idea.**
The hackathon is "Skin AI & eCommerce VTO" and the entry currently ships zero VTO — it's only in the roadmap. Change: wire AR try-on to the 3–5 tinted items in the catalogue (tinted SPF, concealer, tinted moisturizer) with a "See it on your face" button using the scan photo. Why: right now a judge can say "they integrated Skin AI but skipped half the theme"; this closes that hole with the API family Perfect Corp is explicitly showcasing, and it's a natural fit — "this BHA won't work with your skin" plus "here's the tinted SPF that will, on your face."

**3. Wire the breakout moment into commerce — Potential Impact, Design.**
The brief's "right after a bad breakout" moment is currently just a 4-concern check with no follow-through; the Shop list doesn't react to it. Change: after a breakout check, show a "right now" panel — actives to pause (retinoids, acids on irritated skin) and gentle products from the catalogue that fit — even if it's just a filter on the existing ranking. Why: it turns the second brief moment from a measurement gimmick into the full measure→recommend loop, and it's ~one conditional filter on code you already have.

**4. Remove the "Scan first" dead wall on Shop what fits — Design, Impact.**
Section 2 currently says "Scan first (or pick a sample face) to rank these for your skin." A judge who scrolls to it without scanning sees an empty state, not a product experience. Change: pre-rank against Priya by default with a small banner "ranking shown for sample face Priya — scan to get yours." Why: judges rarely scan first; the core retail value must be visible in zero clicks. Same fix for section 3.

**5. Do one real-face mini trial — Potential Impact.**
Every trial datapoint is synthetic, on purpose, and the README says so — which is honest but leaves the Impact criterion's "demo shows it solved" resting on generated images. Change: team lead or a teammate uploads their own selfie, starts a real 2-week trial (even one check-in at week 2), and you show it as "one real user, in progress" beside the synthetic demos. Why: one real scan measured with the real API is worth more credibility than six synthetic check-ins, and it validates your noise-band claim on real capture.

**6. Surface the Skin Simulation before/after images in the product check and trial timeline — Design, Technological Implementation.**
Skin Simulation is a headline API but on the site it reads as a description ("shows a realistic target") rather than a visible artifact. Change: in both demo trials and the product check, show the current photo and the simulated target side by side, labeled. Why: judges should *see* the second YouCam API's output without reading the README; right now its integration is easy to under-credit.

**7. Show the retailer widget as a mock, not a roadmap line — Potential Impact.**
"Fit score on any product page" is the entire retail business case, but it exists only as a sentence. Change: build a static mock product page (one HTML file) with the SkinProof fit-score card embedded for Priya, linked from the Impact section. Why: the B2B value claim becomes demonstrable in one click, and the two-week re-engagement loop ("brings the shopper back") gets a visual home.

**8. Publish per-concern noise bands and per-concern verdict thresholds in the UI — Technological Implementation, Quality of Idea.**
The band is currently one number per face from the worst observed change; a redness band set by a brightness perturbation over-widens the acne band and vice versa. Change: compute and display the band per concern (you have the 6 re-captures per concern already in `noise_test.json`), and show each trial concern's own band on its row. Why: it sharpens your most original idea (noise-respecting verdicts) and preempts the sharpest judge question about false "keep it" verdicts.
